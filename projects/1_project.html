Porous Silicon-on-Paper Quantitative Biosensor

A pump-free flow-through porous silicon optical biosensor for quantitative rapid diagnostic testing

Overview

Conventional porous silicon (PSi) optical biosensors offer label-free and quantitative biomolecular detection, but molecular transport into closed-ended nanoscale pores can limit sensitivity and response time.

Conceptual architecture of the porous silicon-on-paper quantitative biosensing platform.

In this project, we developed a porous silicon-on-paper optical biosensor that combines an open-ended porous silicon free-standing membrane (PSi FSM) with a paper substrate to improve molecular transport through the sensing region.

The paper substrate serves both as a structural support and as a passive fluid-drainage layer, enabling flow through the porous silicon membrane without an external pump or conventional microfluidic channels.

Challenge

Porous silicon provides a large internal surface area and a strong optical response for label-free biosensing. However, conventional PSi sensors fabricated directly on silicon substrates contain closed-ended pores that can limit molecular transport into the active sensing region.

This transport limitation can result in:

  • Slow analyte diffusion into the porous sensing layer
  • Longer assay times
  • Reduced molecular capture within the active region
  • Lower optical signal changes
  • Reduced detection sensitivity

The central engineering challenge was therefore to improve molecular transport through the porous sensing layer while preserving the optical sensing functionality and mechanical integrity of the porous silicon structure.

My Role

My work on this project included:

  • Fabrication of porous silicon free-standing membranes by electrochemical etching
  • Optimization of multilayer porous silicon structures
  • Transfer and integration of PSi membranes with paper substrates
  • Surface oxidation and biofunctionalization
  • Biotin–streptavidin sensing experiments
  • Real-time optical reflectance measurements
  • Effective optical thickness (EOT) analysis
  • Investigation of fluid transport through the porous membrane
  • Comparison of PSi-on-paper and conventional on-substrate PSi sensing performance
  • Experimental troubleshooting and process optimization

The project required integration of materials fabrication, surface chemistry, fluid transport, and optical biosensing into a single sensing platform.

Technical Approach

Three-Layer Porous Silicon Membrane

The sensing platform uses an open-ended three-layer porous silicon free-standing membrane fabricated by electrochemical etching.

The membrane architecture was designed to balance sensing performance and mechanical stability:

  • A thin, high-porosity top layer serves as the active sensing region
  • A thicker middle layer provides mechanical support
  • A thin lower layer creates a symmetric structure that helps reduce internal stress during oxidation and drying

After fabrication, the multilayer porous structure was detached from the silicon substrate through an electrochemical liftoff process.

Surface Functionalization

The free-standing PSi membranes were thermally oxidized to stabilize the porous surface and provide an oxide layer suitable for subsequent chemical modification.

The sensor surface was subsequently functionalized using:

  • APTES surface modification
  • Biotin functionalization
  • Streptavidin as a model target protein

This biotin–streptavidin system was used to evaluate quantitative biomolecular detection and compare the performance of the paper-supported sensor with conventional PSi sensors remaining on silicon substrates.

Passive Flow-Through Transport

A key feature of the platform is the use of paper beneath the open-ended PSi membrane.

The paper provides capillary-driven fluid drainage through the porous membrane, allowing molecules to move through the sensing region rather than relying primarily on diffusion into closed-ended pores.

The assembled device incorporated the PSi membrane, wax-printed paper, a paper top layer, an absorbent pad, and a plastic cartridge designed to maintain contact between the layers.

Importantly, fluid transport was achieved without an external pump or conventional microfluidic channels.

Optical Detection

Molecular transport and binding were monitored through real-time optical reflectance measurements.

Changes in the effective optical thickness (EOT) of the porous silicon sensing layer were determined from the reflectance spectra and used to quantify molecular infiltration and binding.

This enabled label-free, concentration-dependent detection of streptavidin.

Key Results

Optimization of the porous silicon membrane and paper-based fluidic architecture produced substantial improvements in sensor performance.

Optimization of sensor architecture and fluid transport resulted in improved analytical sensitivity and faster sensing response.

Nearly 4× Higher Detection Sensitivity

The open-ended PSi-on-paper platform demonstrated a nearly four-fold improvement in streptavidin detection sensitivity compared with similar porous silicon sensing layers remaining on silicon substrates.

The improvement was attributed to more efficient molecular transport through the open-ended porous membrane.

Quantitative Detection Within 30 Minutes

The sensor demonstrated a concentration-dependent optical response for streptavidin detection within 30 minutes.

This response time was at least two times faster than conventional on-substrate porous silicon sensors.

Pump-Free Fluid Transport

The paper substrate simultaneously:

  • Supported the fragile porous silicon membrane
  • Promoted drainage through the nanoscale pores
  • Enabled passive flow-through molecular transport

This eliminated the need for an external pump or conventional microfluidic channel network.

Project Impact

Project Impact

This work demonstrates how sensor architecture and passive fluid transport can be engineered together to overcome molecular mass-transport limitations in porous optical biosensors.

By integrating an open-ended PSi membrane with a simple paper substrate, the platform provides quantitative, label-free sensing while avoiding the external pumps and microfluidic channel systems previously used for flow-through porous silicon sensing.

The approach establishes a foundation for the development of low-cost quantitative rapid diagnostic platforms based on porous silicon optical sensing.
